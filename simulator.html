FUNCTIONAL DIVERSITY (θstd)

Every replicator has a niche optimum θ — the local environment quality where it replicates fastest. θstd measures how spread out these optima are across the population.

High θstd means replicators occupy genuinely different ecological niches. This is the meaningful diversity metric — not lineage count, which can grow from neutral drift alone.

WHY MIXING MATTERS

Mixing rate D controls how often replicators are teleported to random empty cells anywhere on the grid. Low D preserves isolated communities that locally adapt over time. High D causes constant long-range redistribution that breaks down niche matching.

Key empirical finding: functional diversity peaks at the lowest sampled mixing values. Isolation preserves local specialisation. In this transport regime, the predicted Goldilocks optimum at intermediate D was not observed.
